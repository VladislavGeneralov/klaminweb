# Klamin

Gridless circular sequencer for mobile browsers — a spinning disc instead of a step grid, in the spirit of Theremin's rhythmicon. Drop minerals anywhere on the disc; each one sounds as it passes a fixed playhead while the disc spins. No quantization by default (a toggle is planned).

Current state: a working spike, no build step, no dependencies — almost entirely one file (`index.html`), plus `reverb-processor.js` and `recorder-processor.js`, which have to live on their own since `AudioWorklet` modules are loaded from a URL, not inlined.

Live: **https://vladislavgeneralov.github.io/klaminweb/**

## Why it's built this way

The instrument lives or dies on timing precision and on the visual never lying about the audio, so a few decisions were made deliberately rather than by default:

- **Sound is scheduled on the Web Audio clock, never on `requestAnimationFrame`.** A lookahead scheduler ticks every ~40ms and queues `AudioBufferSourceNode.start(time)` ~150ms ahead on `audioContext`'s own clock. rAF drives only what's drawn on screen.
- **Rotation is a sequence of exponential ramp segments**, not a single speed value. Play/pause spins the disc up/down with real (exponential) inertia, like a turntable motor; the speed fader uses the same mechanism with a much shorter time constant so it feels near-instant. Every new ramp reads the *actual live* speed of whatever ramp is currently active as its own starting point — interrupting a ramp mid-flight never produces an audible or visual jump.
- **Geometry is two fixed reference frames**, not one. Each mineral holds a fixed angle offset from the disc's own rotating zero-mark; the playhead is a fixed radial spoke in screen space that never rotates. A mineral's position is purely angular — radius is spacing only, it has no effect on sound.
- **A mineral's visual *spin* is deliberately not derived from its position.** Position (`thetaOffset`) says where the dot sits; a separate `rotBase` (the disc's own angle at the exact moment it was set down) says how it's rotated — drawn as `discAngle - rotBase`. It keeps whatever orientation it had while being carried (upright, same as the palette icon and the drag preview) and only turns by however much the disc spins *after* being placed. The alternative — deriving rotation from position, so it's "glued flat" at a fixed disc-relative angle — was rejected because it makes the image snap to a new orientation the instant it's dropped, with nothing during the drag to telegraph what that'll be.
- **Any state change (speed, add/remove/move a mineral) cancels not-yet-played scheduled audio and recomputes fresh from the live rotation state.** Dragging a mineral mutes it for the duration of the drag and only triggers a recompute on release; the speed fader recomputes continuously, on every input event, since live tempo control is the point of it.
- **The scheduler's per-mineral `lastK` bookkeeping resets on any *organic* direction flip, not just an explicit recalc.** A reverse ramp doesn't switch direction instantly — it decelerates through zero over ~1s — so the lookahead window's net direction can flip on its own, mid-ramp, with no button press to trigger a `recalcSchedule()` at that exact moment. Reusing `lastK` (built under the old direction's search convention) across that flip was silently skipping crossings right at the transition, worse at higher speed since more crossings fall inside the transition window. Fixed by comparing this tick's direction to the last tick's and resetting the search state (not the already-queued audio) on any change. Separately, a crossing whose computed time lands a hair before `now` (Newton/floating-point noise, most visible for a mineral sitting right at the search boundary) is now clamped into the playable future instead of silently dropped — that was eating the trigger for minerals near the zero mark specifically.
- **`recalcSchedule` re-derives the schedule immediately instead of leaving the refill to the next timer tick.** It's called on every fader-drag `pointermove` with no throttling (also from the handle's jog-wheel drag) — it wipes `lastK` and any stale-timed pending audio, but on its own schedules nothing new; that used to wait for `schedulerTick`'s own `setInterval` (`SCHED_INTERVAL_MS`, 40ms). A fast, repeated drag reopens that gap on every move, and if a mineral's crossing happened to land inside one of those gaps, the next tick's `gNow` was already past it — `kMin` correctly excludes anything already-passed, so the trigger was silently dropped even though nothing looked wrong visually right up until that moment (it just hadn't been re-scheduled yet, not that it was scheduled wrong). `recalcSchedule` now calls `schedulerTick()` directly at the end instead of waiting — safe to call this often since the `lastK` bookkeeping is inherently idempotent, so the regular timer tick a few ms later just sees the fresh state and does nothing extra.
- **Visual feedback for a trigger (the flash) is never driven by the scheduler.** The scheduler only queues audio. The render loop independently re-derives, every frame, from the same closed-form angle formula, whether a mineral just crossed the playhead — otherwise the flash would visibly lead the sound by the whole lookahead window (~150ms), which is the classic bug in this kind of system.
- **Layout is sized once from the smallest guaranteed viewport (`100svh`), not measured ad hoc**, with a sanity-check retry (via `ResizeObserver`) if an early reading comes back implausibly small. Combined with `touch-action`/`overscroll-behavior` locks, the page never needs to scroll. Even on desktop the app renders inside a fixed portrait frame — it's a phone instrument first.
- **Mineral sounds are real recorded samples**, loaded via `fetch` + `decodeAudioData`, decoded before the user ever touches the screen. There are 22 mineral slots — no bank toggle anymore; each of the 22 always has its own fixed image (`minerals/N.webp`) and sound (`samples/N.wav`, flat, no per-mineral subfolder), so a mineral already placed on the disc can never change underneath the player, and all 22 sounds are simultaneously available to combine. `AudioContext` itself is created and unlocked exactly once, on the first play tap, and is never suspended again afterward.
- **The palette is a 2x11 grid**, windowed to however many columns actually fit the screen at a fixed 50px button size (not forced to exactly 5 — a wide screen just shows more) — top row minerals 1-11, bottom row 12-22, aligned into columns. Modest side padding keeps buttons off the very screen edge, and the clip viewport itself carries 6px of extra padding top and bottom so a button's tap-flash glow has room to render instead of being cut off flush against the clip edge (every button sits equally close to that edge vertically, unlike horizontally where only whatever's mid-scroll is ever near a side). The rest is reached by sliding sideways from anywhere on the palette, buttons included: a button's own tap/drag/long-press state machine (below) treats a mostly-horizontal first movement as "slide the palette" rather than "drag to place," so the same touch that would place a mineral can instead pan the view depending on which way it first moves. A brown bar under the palette shows scroll position, its width a live fraction (visible/total) of the actual measured track, not a hardcoded ratio.
- **Palette buttons disambiguate tap, drag, and long-press from one shared pointer-tracking state**, not three independent listeners. A tap (released before a 600ms timer, moved under a 4px jitter threshold) flashes the button gold (a `drop-shadow`, since it's a background-image button, not a flat fill) and previews that mineral's sound — `audioContext.resume()` is awaited before playback starts, since it's async and starting playback before it actually resolves can silently drop the very first tap. Crossing the movement threshold before the timer cancels it and falls through to the existing drag-to-place flow; the timer firing first opens a long-press menu instead — numbers 16 down to 1 top-to-bottom, then a red "✕" below 1, tap a number to scatter that many copies of the mineral evenly around the disc: only the first sits exactly on the zero mark, the rest just divide the full circle evenly (`i * 2π/n`, continuous — not snapped to the 16-slot quantize grid, even if quantize happens to be on), each one starting at its own random visual rotation independent of its (still exactly even) position. Movement after the menu opens is ignored (no accidental drag); the menu itself behaves like an ordinary popup afterward — tap a number or the ✕ (a quick flash, gold or red respectively, confirms the pick before the menu closes), tap outside to dismiss. Picking a number again for the same mineral replaces its previous multiplier-placed set rather than adding more on top; the ✕ clears just that mineral's multiplier-placed stones, leaving hand-placed ones untouched — both tracked via a `viaMultiplier` flag on the disc entry.
- **Quantization is a toggle, not a mode baked into placement.** Off (default): a mineral's angle is whatever you dropped it at — fully free. On: 16 evenly spaced radial slots (relative to the disc's own rotating zero-mark, not the screen) are always visible as a faint dark grid, turning gold while quantize is active; a dropped mineral's true position is snapped to the nearest slot immediately (so audio timing is correct from the first frame), and only the *visual* dot animates from your drop point to the slot over ~0.2s.
- **Radius never means "slot taken."** Sound only depends on angle, so several minerals can legitimately share one quantize line at different radii — that's not a collision, just a stack on the same spoke. A real collision (same line *and* close radius, or close pixel proximity in freeform) resolves by nudging — freeform slides the angle to whichever side is closer to where you dropped; quantize keeps the snapped angle exactly and slides the *radius* along that same line instead. The "fly back" reject is reserved for the dead zone now, nothing else.
- **The quantize "catch" glow is a plain pixel distance, not an angle.** While quantize is on, the drag ring turns gold once you're within one mineral's diameter (in screen pixels, computed as arc length at the drag's own radius) of a line — snapping itself always finds the nearest line regardless, this is purely a "you're about to catch here" cue, deliberately not lit for the whole wedge.

## Mobile performance pass

A real issue found and fixed: `canvas.getBoundingClientRect()` was being called from several hot paths — every rAF frame while any mineral was mid-drag or mid-snap-animation, and on every raw `pointermove` event during a drag (which can fire faster than rAF on some touchscreens). That's a forced synchronous layout read each time. Since the layout here is fixed (no scroll, sized off `svh`), the canvas only actually moves on a real resize — its position is now cached (`canvasRect`) once in `applySize()` and on `resize`/`orientationchange`, and every other call site reads the cache instead.

Smaller fixes in the same pass: `ctx2d.shadowBlur` (a real per-draw cost on some mobile canvas backends) is now only set while a mineral is actually flashing or lifted, not as a constant low-level glow on every idle mineral every frame; the handful of `rgba(...)` strings that never change frame-to-frame (grid color, zero mark, playhead, catch rings) are computed once at load instead of re-parsed from hex every frame; the `disk.some(closure)` flash check became a plain loop to drop the per-frame closure allocation.

Checked and not a concern at the original 9-mineral scale: mineral photos are 410×410, ~150-225KB each; the scheduler's `setInterval` tick is O(minerals) with a handful of Newton-Raphson iterations, negligible; `ctx2d.save()/rotate()/restore()` per mineral (for the disc-relative spin) is a normal, cheap canvas operation. The palette has since grown to 20 minerals (roughly double the image payload, plus audio once samples 10-20 are recorded) — worth re-checking total load size once those samples exist, none of the *per-frame* costs above scale badly with mineral count so this is a one-time-load concern, not a runtime one.

## Samples page

Opened from the SAMPLES corner button (bottom-left disc corner), a near-fullscreen overlay (same absolute-within-`#app` footprint as the reverb panel, just full height) listing all 22 minerals at once, scrolled — with its own vertical scrollbar styled and behaving exactly like the palette's (`#paletteScrollbar`): a proportional, draggable thumb kept in sync with the list's native scroll, not a replacement for it. Each row: thumbnail, a waveform (peaks from `originalBuffer`'s channel 0, drawn once per row and cached until the material changes, mirrored within the trim window whenever reversed so the picture always matches play order) with two draggable trim handles and a moving playhead during preview, duration, four round icon buttons — PLAY (triangle/bars, same swap as the main transport button), REV (a "<>" pair), REC (a plain dot, turns solid red while capturing), WAV (an upload arrow) — and two thin horizontal sliders, PITCH (0.5x-2x, logarithmic, centered on 1x) and VOLUME (0-100%, defaults to 80%). Pitch/volume are real per-mineral voice parameters, not just preview-only knobs — `playMineralAt` applies both (`playbackRate` and a per-play `GainNode`) whenever that mineral triggers on the disc, same live-update path as a trimmed/reversed buffer.

Trim/reverse are **not destructive** — each mineral slot keeps `originalBuffer` (the shipped `samples/N.wav`, or whatever was last recorded/uploaded) untouched, plus `trimStart`/`trimEnd`/`reversed`; the actual working buffer (what `playMineralAt` reads, so a mineral already on the disc updates live) is rebuilt from those three numbers on every edit. Recording or uploading replaces `originalBuffer` wholesale and resets trim/reverse back to "whole thing, forward," matching how a real re-record would work. Nothing is written to disk and nothing persists — a page reload always returns to the shipped 22 samples, by design (no save/load exists anywhere in the app).

Recording captures raw PCM via a tiny `AudioWorkletProcessor` (`recorder-processor.js`) rather than `MediaRecorder`, sidestepping container/codec differences across mobile browsers (notably inconsistent on Safari) that would need decoding back to a buffer anyway before trim/reverse could apply. The length cap is 4 seconds — comfortable headroom over the longest shipped sample (`15.wav`, 3.315s); an uploaded file longer than that is silently truncated to the cap, same as a recording auto-stopping there.

- No save/load — sessions are ephemeral by design (this includes the samples page's edits).

## Visual style

Fixed token set, defined once in `:root` and read into a JS `THEME` object at load (`getComputedStyle`) so canvas drawing can never drift from the CSS values — there is exactly one place that defines what "gold" or "panel" means.

| Token | Value | Used for |
|---|---|---|
| `--bg` | `#000000` | Page background, disc's center hub |
| `--panel` | `#171310` | The play/fader bar (top) and the palette bar (bottom) — same tone, deliberately |
| `--ink` / `--ink-dim` | `#ece6da` / `#8c8375` | Body text color (mostly unused — see below) / toggle borders and label text, always, regardless of on/off state |
| `--accent` | `#e3a458` | Fader thumb, disc-zero mark, valid-drop ring |
| `--accent-dim` | `#7a5a34` | Play button fill |
| `--edge` | `#0e0b08` | All borders and deep insets (fader track, mineral/button outlines) |
| `--gold` | `#d4af37` | Disc rim, dead-zone ring, quantize grid, playhead marker, toggle knobs — always gold, on or off, on/off is shown by the knob's position alone |
| `--vinyl` | `#241608` | Disc body |
| `--danger` | `#c4503c` | Invalid-drop ring |
| `--ivory` | `#f2ede2` | Fixed playhead spoke |

Rules that go with the tokens, not just the values:

- No gradients anywhere, CSS or canvas — including soft inset-shadow bevels that only *simulate* a gradient (tried once on the buttons, read as dated "2000s glossy" style, removed). Flat fills, hard-edged borders only.
- No visible text labels except the small caption under each round corner button (REVERB, CLEAR, SAMPLES, QUANTIZE) and the "Q" glyph inside one of them — added deliberately once a control needed to identify itself outside the context of something labeled it sits next to. REV has none at all: a right-pointing triangle above its track and a left-pointing one below stand in for "forward"/"reverse" instead of text. Text overall is light-weight (400), not bold, and small — 6-14px depending on role, never louder than the controls it labels.
- The fader and REV share one visual language: a bordered rectangular track with a rectangular knob — nothing round-and-thin like a native slider. The four disc-corner controls are round instead, like the play button, all built off one shared `.roundCtrlBtn` shape.
- **Toggle rule, established once and meant to hold for every toggle added later:** the track's *height* — border included, `box-sizing: border-box` is set globally — matches the fader's own 30px height; width is narrow (12px) and independent of that. The label sits *below* the track, absolutely positioned out of normal flow — so a toggle's track is what aligns with neighboring controls, never nudged off-center by its own label's width or presence. Only REV (inline next to the fader) uses this shape now — quantize moved to a round button.
- **The disc's four corners each carry one round control** (`.roundCtrlBtn`, same shape/size as the play button, not the toggle track, each with its own small label underneath): REVERB top-left, directly under Play; CLEAR (✕) top-right, directly under REV above it; SAMPLES bottom-left — reserved, does nothing yet, icon is an outline-only (no fill) upward triangle, a small inline SVG since the border-trick used for the app's other triangles can't produce a hollow shape; QUANTIZE (Q) bottom-right, turning gold while it's on instead of using CLEAR's momentary press-flash, since its color has to hold as a state. CLEAR only acts after a confirm dialog ("CLEAR SEQUENCE?", then CANCEL / CLEAR); tapping the dark backdrop cancels too, same as tapping outside the long-press number menu.
- **REVERB opens a full-width panel instead of a centered dialog** — spanning the full width of the app frame (not the raw browser viewport — this is a phone instrument first even on desktop) and roughly as tall as the top controls bar. While open, the button swaps in place — same circle, same slot — from the three-rings icon/"REVERB" label to a left-pointing triangle/"EXIT"; tapping it again or tapping anywhere outside the panel (same outside-pointerdown-listener pattern as the long-press number menu) closes it and swaps the button back. The panel holds one slider, `.faderTrackLook`/`.faderThumbLook` — a shared class pulled out of the speed fader's own styling so this one is guaranteed pixel-identical to it, not just visually similar — sitting inside a row (`#reverbPanelRow`) that mirrors `#controls`' own padding/gap/spacer structure (18px sides, 16px gaps, a 52px spacer standing in for Play and a 15px one for REV) so it lands at the exact horizontal position of the speed fader above it. Defaults to 0 (off) and holds its value across opening/closing the panel — the panel toggling visibility never touches it.
- **Reverb is `reverb-processor.js` ported directly from SUAQ26** — the same hand-rolled Dattorro plate (Lexicon-style figure-8 tank) `AudioWorkletProcessor` SUAQ26 uses, not something built for Klamin. A `ConvolverNode`-based engine was tried first (native, FFT-based, in principle cheaper than per-sample JS DSP) but was abandoned after real, repeated bugs: a fixed impulse buffer fundamentally fights live decay-time control, and every fix (a forced tail fade so a CPU-capped buffer didn't end on an audible hard stop, crossfading between two convolvers instead of ever reassigning one's `.buffer` — which resets its internal state, a documented Web Audio quirk — then picking the crossfade target more carefully so a fast back-and-forth drag didn't land on a still-ringing slot) was working around that same fixed-buffer mismatch, and it was still misbehaving under fast slider movement. Dattorro is a real feedback delay network: `decay`/`damping`/`wet` are plain `AudioParam`s that ramp smoothly via `setTargetAtTime` with no buffer to reassign and no internal state to reset, so that whole class of bug can't happen here. The one REVERB slider drives `decay` (linear map of the knob, range 0.3-0.85 — Dattorro's own feedback coefficient, not seconds, kept off 1.0 to avoid runaway sustain) and the send level (a quadratic curve, `t*t` — more resolution at the low end, a dramatic jump near the top, per user request over a plain linear mix) together; `damping` is fixed at 0.3 (tames the top end in the feedback loop — the processor's own built-in default, 0.005, is close to no damping at all, too bright for percussive mineral hits), and `wet`/`dry` on the node itself are fixed at 1/0 since dry is already handled by each mineral's own direct-to-destination connection — the node only ever needs to hand back its wet signal in full, with `reverbSendGain` as the sole level control. A fixed 100Hz highpass sits ahead of the node on the send only, so the tail doesn't build up low-end mud; dry signal is untouched. The real tradeoff versus the abandoned `ConvolverNode` approach: an `AudioWorkletProcessor` does per-sample JS DSP continuously for as long as it's connected, so unlike a convolver (which can sit idle for free) this one is only ever wired into the graph (`reverbHighpass -> reverbNode -> destination`) while the knob is above 0, disconnected again the instant it returns to 0 — mirroring how each mineral's send tap already only connects above 0. Loading the worklet module (`audioCtx.audioWorklet.addModule`) is async, so the node doesn't exist yet for a brief window after the panel's first-ever open; `applyReverbAmount` no-ops until it does, and the module's own load callback re-syncs to whatever the knob is holding once it's ready.
- **Dragging an existing mineral off the disc just glows** — same `shadowBlur` treatment as a trigger-flash or any other lifted state, no separate ring drawn around it. There used to be one (`drawCatchRing`, gold when snapped to a quantize line, red/accent for invalid/valid position) but it read as a redundant second highlight on top of the glow already there; removed rather than kept as unused visual noise. The from-palette drag preview (`#dragGhost`, DOM-based, `valid`/`caught`/`invalid` CSS classes) is untouched — it's a different preview mechanism for placing a brand-new mineral, not repositioning one already on the disc.
- **The palette's scroll indicator is itself directly draggable** (`#paletteScrollbarThumb`, mainly a desktop affordance — the usual scrollbar-thumb habit), with a deliberately tight hit zone: just the thumb's own rendered box, no padded-out grab area around it.
- Layout is play/fader bar at the top, disc in the middle, mineral palette at the bottom — deliberately, not the more obvious "palette above the disc." A mobile browser's own chrome (address bar, home indicator) tends to encroach on the very bottom of the viewport, so the palette — reach down and grab a mineral, tolerant of a few pixels being covered — sits there instead of the play/fader controls, which need to stay reliably reachable.
- The disc itself reads as a vinyl record on purpose: dark brown body, gold rim, gold ring at the dead-zone boundary, black center dot.
- Two matching arrow markers, not lines: the zero mark (inside the dead-zone ring, rotates with the disc) and the playhead marker (outside the rim, fixed in screen space, pointing inward) are both filled, both gold — the zero mark long and narrow, the playhead noticeably shorter and squatter, so they read as a pair without being identical.
- A control's border must contrast with its own fill, not just with the panel behind it — `--edge`-on-`--edge` (fader/button borders) is invisible-by-design since those sit on a lighter surface; anything that itself sits on a dark ground (the toggles) borders in `--ink-dim` instead.
- Minerals are real photos (`minerals/N.webp`, transparent-background cutouts, lossy WebP q85 — roughly 1/6th the size of the original PNGs with no visible loss at the sizes they're ever shown), not flat color dots — in the palette, on the disc, and in the drag preview. The flat `MINERAL_COLORS` palette still exists as a fallback (a photo that hasn't loaded, or is missing) and as the tint behind each mineral's glow/shadow. The original PNGs live in the gitignored `minerals/_original_backup/` in case a re-encode at different settings is ever needed.
- The dragged-from-palette preview is a real DOM element (`#dragGhost`, `position: fixed`), not drawn on canvas — canvas is clipped to `discWrap`'s box and can't render above the palette panel while a new mineral is still being dragged over it. Everything else (on-disk minerals, the snap-into-place animation) stays canvas-drawn since it never needs to leave the disc's own bounds.

## Running it

Opening `index.html` directly via `file://` breaks sample loading — `fetch()` of local files is blocked by the browser. Double-click `start.lnk` (or `start.bat`): it starts a local static server (`python -m http.server 8080`) and opens `http://localhost:8080`. For testing on an actual phone, open the machine's LAN IP on port 8080 from the phone, same wifi network.
